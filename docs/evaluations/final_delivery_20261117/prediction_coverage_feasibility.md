# Forecast population coverage

The completed temporal study already forecast the full eligible population. No additional same-window study is recommended.

The historical graph contains **20,412 proteins**. The captured April 10–November 4, 2026 reference yields **3,300 eligible human Swiss-Prot annotation-gainer proteins** across the three Gene Ontology aspects and all native knowledge classes. The fixed-ontology reference contains 1,690 proteins, all within that 3,300-protein union. Of the forecasts, 3,297 proteins occur in the historical graph and three are retained explicitly despite being absent from it. This is a complete eligible reference population under the frozen policy, rather than a random sample of the graph.

Eligibility requires exact reviewed-human accessions and identical full sequences at both source epochs. The unchanged source helpers admit eleven experimental evidence codes plus curator inference (IC) and traceable author statements (TAS). Ontology, source-subset and generic-binding rules are preserved. Propagated-term gains can reflect ontology changes. Later annotation does not necessarily mean a newly performed experiment.

The primary scores use a narrower task: **additional biological-process annotations for proteins already annotated in that same aspect at T0** (partial knowledge, or PK). Its native evaluator frame contains 1,954 protein rows, 322 with residual-positive labels after masking. The other 1,632 can still contribute false positives. The fixed-ontology primary frame has 319 protein rows, all with residual-positive labels. All 3,300 forecasts were retained for the full multi-aspect assessment. These denominators do not mean that all 20,412 graph proteins received a scientific accuracy score.

A known protein can legitimately be predicted for a later function. Pre-cutoff labels are allowed context and are masked from the partial-knowledge endpoint. The project's stacker additionally excluded exact accessions, identical sequences and identical first 1,022 residues across its 4,040 fitting, 797 consumed-validation and 3,300 final proteins. That does not establish independence from the base models' original training. Scoring more proteins on annotations already used for fitting, validation or pre-cutoff context would change the task. Proteins without later qualifying annotations cannot simply be counted as biological negatives.

The strongest existing control already forecasts the same 3,300 targets from the same graph using the complete eligible April 10 curated annotation repertoire. It includes legal known-at-T0 annotations on query proteins and excludes later source information. Learning's additional primary benefit over that refreshed control remains uncertain. No missing control availability is resolved by expanding the forecast list.

Compatible frozen models could technically generate additional predictions after feature preparation. Existing operators deliberately enforce the 3,300-target frame. The earlier fresh sequence-model pass for 1,716 proteins took about 22 minutes 39 seconds. More inference would consume compute without adding eligible same-window positives. Another temporal accuracy assessment requires a genuinely unconsumed later label population and a new predeclared source boundary. No such population is established by the current artifacts.

This conclusion follows the [official CAFA temporal assessment](https://biofunctionprediction.org/cafa/) and the [ProtBoost paper, sections 4.9–4.10](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04529v1), which distinguish the sequence forecast pool from the proteins eventually eligible for assessment. The project remains a retrospective temporal adaptation, not a new prospective CAFA submission.
